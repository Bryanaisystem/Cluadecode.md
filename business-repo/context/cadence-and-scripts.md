# Outreach Cadence & Call Script

## Day cadence
Day 1 / 3 / 5 / 7 — applies **only while there's no response**. The moment they reply, stop the day-cadence entirely and branch:
- Soft interest → move to the booking micro-cadence
- Not interested → one polite close ("Before I close this out, is there someone else who owns this, or is it a firm no overall?"). If firm/hostile: close immediately. Don't defend, don't pursue, don't argue.

## No-show recovery (the one exception to normal cadence rules)
A no-show is a timing problem until proven otherwise — most are missed calendar alerts, not rejection.
1. **Same-ish window** — give an easy out + new slot: "Looks like today got away from you — want to run it now, or rebook for later today?"
2. **Next day (final professional attempt)** — reschedule within 24–48h: "I can do time A or time B tomorrow — which works?"
3. **If that doesn't land** — one line, step back: "If it's not a priority right now, tell me what month to circle back and I'll leave you alone until then."
4. If that fails too → nurture (follow up later, e.g. on a new AI-awareness-cycle trigger).

**Never:** message 5x in a day, write paragraphs, do "just checking in" for a week straight.

## Call script structure
Diagnose → quantify → qualify → value-frame.
- **Diagnose:** reflect back what you heard before moving on ("let me make sure I get this right — what you've tried, why it matters now..."). Being understood is the gateway to being sold.
- **Quantify:** see context/offer-blueprint.md — turn the pain into a real number using the 4 lanes (time/money/risk/reputation), without pressure tactics.
- **Qualify / value-frame:** confirm they're the buyer, confirm the mechanism lands, move toward the decision-maker conversation — the single destination every outreach channel exists to reach.

## The core discipline
Progress is a chain, not a single metric: leads → prototype → outreach → sales (decision-maker conversation) → payment → delivery/retention. Beginners obsess over payment and skip/half-ass steps 1–4 — payment is a consequence of the earlier steps done correctly, not the starting point.

## Anti-Spam Law (6 rules — not advice, rules)
Protects reputation and deliverability. Breaking these is what gets you flagged/blocked.

1. **One prospect = one touch max per day.** No double-messaging the same day — that's
   not follow-up, that's harassment energy.
2. **Written or calls: hard ceiling of 4 touches across days 1–7** for your primary
   pathway.
3. **IRL: max 3 touches in 7 days.** High-trust but high-risk — overdo it and you become
   "that guy," not memorable.
4. **Do not mix pathways in week one.** Written-first stays written-first all week.
   Mixing looks like panic and kills response rate.
5. **Escalate once, only** — switch pathway a single time after day 7. After that, move
   on like an operator managing a pipeline, don't keep poking.
6. **Day 14 = nurture or close.** No "day 27 check-in." We run sequences, not harassment.

**Core rule: we earn access, not replies.**

## Pathway Selector — how to choose written vs. calls vs. IRL
1. **Can I reach them directly in writing, reliably?** Yes → written first.
2. Do I have a direct, reliable phone number in a phone-friendly niche (local service,
   clinic)? Yes → calls first.
3. Do I have proximity or a natural in-person reason? Yes → IRL first.
4. If all three are no → default to written first and go improve lead data quality
   before pushing more outreach volume.

Choose **written** when you need context, proof, and a clean intro (attach a
prototype). Choose **calls** when you want a fast signal within the week and can
navigate a gatekeeper quickly. Choose **IRL** only when there's a natural reason to
visit — forced IRL backfires and reads as reputation damage, not memorable.

**The biggest mistake:** treating a channel as proven when you have no real pipeline
relationship with the prospect — never let a prospect sense you're specifically short
on leads.

## What counts as "engaged" (written pathway)
Counts: "send it over" / "show me"; any objection (pricing, timing — still engagement);
they give a better contact method or ask about scheduling; even "not now" counts if they
give a real timeline.
Does NOT count: a read receipt with no reply; a one-word brush-off with no path forward;
a generic "thanks" with no movement.

## Calls-first and IRL-first cadence (condensed — use if the niche is phone- or
visit-friendly; written is the default for this business)

**Calls-first, day 1/3/5/7:** Day 1 = call + recap email. Day 3 = call two. Day 5 = call
three. Day 7 = close-loop email ("I'll assume now isn't a priority" energy). Goal on
call one: convert to a booking, get transferred to the decision-maker, or get direct
contact details/a scheduled time. If gatekept, stay calm — request the right person and
the best time to reach them. If day 7 closes with nothing, the signal is your list is
too thin — fix the list, not your effort.

**IRL-first, day 1/2/5:** Day 1 = first visit (goal: best contact method, an
instruction to send something, or a revisit invitation — not a full pitch). Day 2 =
one-line follow-up referencing the visit, asking for the meeting or right person. Day 5
= optional call/revisit, only with a clean reason (a direct contact was given, or you
have something specific to show). Skip it if forced. Exit cleanly if there's no movement
— repeated IRL touches become personal fast and burn trust.
